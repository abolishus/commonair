# Code of Conduct

CommonAir is built and owned by its members. We want everyone who contributes,
from first-time reporters to long-time maintainers, to be able to do their best
work without being harassed, demeaned, or shut out. This document says what we
expect, what we will not accept, and what happens when someone crosses the
line.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with respect, including when you disagree with them.
- Assume good faith. Ask before concluding that someone meant harm.
- Critique ideas and code, not the people who wrote them.
- Be patient with newcomers and people working in a second language.
- Accept feedback gracefully, and give it in a way you would want to receive.
- Credit others' work. Do not take credit for what you did not do.
- Respect people's time, privacy, and stated boundaries.
- When you make a mistake, own it, apologize, and fix it.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning comments about a person's identity, including
  race, ethnicity, nationality, caste, religion, sex, gender identity or
  expression, sexual orientation, disability, age, appearance, body size,
  class, or experience level.
- Sexual language, imagery, or attention that is unwelcome or out of context.
- Threats or incitement of violence or self-harm.
- Sharing someone's private information (doxxing), or threatening to.
- Stalking, unwanted following, or continuing contact after being asked to stop.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews, meetings, or events.
- Deliberately misgendering someone or using a name they no longer use.
- Retaliating against anyone who reports a concern or takes part in an
  investigation.
- Encouraging or coordinating any of the above.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull
requests, discussions, chat channels, mailing lists, video calls, and in-person
events. It also applies to contributors in the platform's own community spaces
for contributors, and when you represent the project elsewhere (for example,
speaking about CommonAir at a conference or posting from a project account).

Behavior outside these spaces may be considered when it creates a real risk to
the safety of community members.

Viewers and streamers using the platform itself are covered by the platform's
community guidelines and trust and safety policies, which are separate from
this document.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, write to
**conduct@commonair.tv**. Include what happened, when and where, who was
involved, and any links or screenshots. You do not need to be the person
affected to report.

Reports are kept confidential. We share details only with the people handling
the report, and only as much as needed. We will not reveal who reported without
their consent, except where the law requires it.

If the conduct involves someone who would normally handle reports, say so in
your message; it will be routed to members with no conflict.

## Who handles reports

Reports are handled by the conduct committee appointed by the cooperative's
board. Any committee member with a conflict of interest (they are involved,
they are close to someone involved, or they have a reason to be biased) must
recuse themselves from that case. If too few members remain, the board appoints
a substitute for the case.

The committee acknowledges reports within three business days, investigates,
hears from the people involved where it is safe to do so, and decides on a
response. Decisions are explained to the person reported and, where
appropriate, to the reporter.

## Enforcement

The committee chooses a response proportional to the harm, the pattern of
behavior, and the person's willingness to change. Responses usually follow
this ladder, but serious violations can skip steps.

1. **Correction.** A private note explaining what was wrong and what is
   expected. A public apology may be requested.
2. **Warning.** A formal warning with stated consequences for continued
   behavior, and a period of restricted interaction with the people involved.
3. **Temporary ban.** No participation in project spaces for a set period.
   Contributions may be paused or reassigned.
4. **Permanent ban.** No participation in project spaces, indefinitely.

A person subject to a decision may appeal once, in writing, to the board. Board
members with a conflict recuse themselves from the appeal.
